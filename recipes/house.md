# The house standard

Every clip follows these rules, whatever its kind. They come from the short clips that worked: the format whose
clips reached 10,000 to 19,000 views, where the ones before it stayed under 1,100. Where this recipe says more about
its own kind, both hold; the person's own words win over both.

## The first two seconds

- Open on the clip's strongest line: the question it answers, the claim, the result or the peak. Never open on a
  greeting, a "today I will show you", a breath or a pause: viewers decide in the first two seconds, and a slow
  start loses them before the point.
- When the strongest line comes after a setup, start on that line and leave the setup out, as long as the clip still
  makes sense to someone who saw nothing before it.
- The title is on the picture from the first frame and tells the viewer why to keep watching.

## Cutting

- Each piece starts on the first word of a sentence and ends after its last word. Never cut words out so that
  someone seems to say what they did not say.
- One clip, one complete thought: a question and its answer with the numbers said, a step and its result, a story
  and its point.
- Nothing holds still for long: a still screen, such as a slide or a page, gets a slow push, and a number shows on
  the picture when it is said.

## Words on the picture

- The title and any big words are for a stranger scrolling past who knows nothing about the topic: everyday words,
  no jargon, no abbreviation they may not know. Say what the thing does for them: "บอทเทรด", not "EA"; "บัญชีทดลอง",
  not "Demo"; "ไม่มีจุดผิดเลย", not "compile ผ่าน". Captions stay the speaker's own words, jargon included.
- A title starts from what the viewer wants or what bothers them, in the words they would use, not from the name of
  a feature.
- A number on the picture is exactly the one on the screen or the one said. When the hearing and the screen
  disagree, the screen wins. Never round, convert or guess a number.
- Never a middle dot (·) between phrases: a space, or a new line.
- Captions are plain when a person talks on camera. Words lit one by one only over a voice-over or a song: a moving
  colour under a talking face is a second place to look.
- Words never cover each other, a face, the product or a price.
- No sponsor's or brand's logo or name on the picture unless the person asks for it: a short cut loses the context
  that keeps it fair to them. A shop's or a place's name as its own sign writes it says where the viewer is, and
  may be named there.

## The post

- Never name another app, or use a tag that belongs to one, in the title, the post text, the hashtags or the words
  on the picture: the same file goes to every app. To point to the full video, write "ดูคลิปเต็มในลิงก์โปรไฟล์", or
  "full video: link in profile" in the clip's language.
- Five hashtags: first three about what the viewer is looking for or struggling with, because the apps show the
  first three; then two about the topic or the channel. Each one is about this clip: a tag any clip of its kind
  could carry says nothing.

## Hidden and kept

- The person's own name, on the screen or said, is kept: it is theirs to show.
- Hidden for as long as it shows: e-mail addresses, phone numbers, real account, card and ID numbers, other people's
  names on a screen, and folder paths that hold a user's name.

## Sound

- A game clip keeps the game's sound and the player's voice, with no music.

## Before handing over

- Watch the first two seconds once more as a stranger would: would they stop scrolling?
- Read every word on the picture as that stranger: is there a word they would not know? Say it another way.
