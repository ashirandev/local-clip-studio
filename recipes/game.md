# Game stream

After the clip, the viewer has felt the moment: what led up to it, the peak, and the streamer's reaction.

## Finding the moment

- Rank the stretches by how loud they are against the minutes around them, how much the screen changes, and
  sounds such as laughter, a scream or a hit.
- Start a few seconds before the peak, so the viewer sees what led to it.
- The streamer's voice matters more than the game's music.

## Rules

- Never a cutscene alone: the clip needs the player playing or reacting.
- Blur the stream's chat box, so viewers' names are not shown.
- Captions are the streamer's own words.

## The clip

- Layout: the game as a card across the middle, the face camera in a circle above it, the title on top.
- The title says what happens, in the clip's language, without giving the ending away.
- Look: vivid. One slow push on the build-up, ending on the peak.

## Checked at the end

- no viewer's name can be read
- the captions are words that were said
- the length is inside the Reel limit, and the clip does not start silent or black
