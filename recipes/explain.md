# Tutorial or screen explainer

After the clip, the viewer has learned one thing they can use: one tip, one step, or what one feature does.

## Finding the moment

- One moment is one complete idea: it opens on the line that names what the viewer will get (the problem, the
  question or the result), never on a greeting or a "today I will show you", and ends after the result is on
  screen, not in the middle of a step.
- Rank the stretches by how much the screen changes while the person talks: a result appearing, a page changing, a
  before and after.
- Skip waiting: loading, typing a long text, silence while a program thinks.
- On money, trading or investing, take a stretch that teaches what something is or what to prepare, never one that
  tells the viewer how much to invest, how to split their money, or what to buy.

## Rules

- The program reads the screen and hides every line with an email address, a phone number, a card or ID number, a
  key, an account number or a password, for as long as it shows. It cannot tell a name: look at the pictures for a
  customer's name and blur it.
- Captions are the person's own words.
- A name (a program, a site, a product) is written as the screen shows it, even where it was heard as another word:
  the screen says Eaglet, so it is Eaglet, never the "Egret" that was heard. Never offer to change a name the screen
  shows.

## The clip

- Layout: the screen as a card across the middle and the face camera in a circle above it, even when the person asks
  for "a clip of me talking": the screen is what they explain. The face layout only when there is no screen to show,
  or the person asks for their face alone, such as "only my face".
- The title says what the viewer will be able to do, in the clip's language, in a few words; a number in it must be
  one that was said.
- Captions on, plain: the words explain the screen.
- Pictures: with no face camera the space above the card is empty, so when the person names a thing a picture can
  show (gold bars, a price going up, a shop counter), a picture of it over the top on the second it is said, 1 or 2
  in a clip, each a new thing. Never a picture of the screen itself or of words.
- Look: none. One slow push on the result when it appears.
- Sounds: a ding when the result appears, and nothing more.

## Checked at the end

- nothing private can be read on the screen
- the captions are words that were said
- numbers on screen were said
- the length is inside the Reel limit, and the clip does not start silent or black
