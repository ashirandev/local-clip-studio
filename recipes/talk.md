# Podcast or interview

After the clip, the viewer has heard one complete thought: a question and its answer, a story with its point, or a
joke and the laugh after it.

## Finding the moment

- Rank the stretches by how loud they are against the minutes around them: laughter and raised voices mark the
  moments people share.
- A moment starts on the question or the first words of the story, not in the middle of a sentence, and ends after
  the point lands, with the other person's reaction.
- What is said matters more than how the picture changes.
- A clip runs as long as its thought needs, up to 90 s: the length guessed is where to start, not where to stop.
  When a look ends while someone is still telling the story or answering, look at the next stretch before you
  propose, and end where they finish and the others react (Tarm 10-30, on EP 0 cut at 45 s in the middle of an
  answer: "ตัดห้วนเกินไป มันยังไม่จบ").
- Never the episode's opening, where the people greet the viewer and say who is who: a viewer scrolling past
  does not know them yet, and a list of names gives no reason to stay. A story with its point, a question and
  its answer, or a joke and its laugh does.
- Tell the person why the moment is worth watching by what is said in it, never by how loud it was: "the loudest
  stretch" tells them nothing; "where she tells why they switched from slots to cards" does.

## Rules

- A viewer's name or handle shown on the screen, such as on a comment card read out in a Q&A, is hidden.

## The clip

- Layout: cut between the faces (talk): whoever speaks fills the frame, with a short cut to the one listening for a
  reaction. When they laugh or talk together the frame stays on the one who spoke last (Tarm, 10-31:
  "นั่งคุยเอาแบบซูมเข้าคนที่พูด"), and everyone together only when the person asks. Faces in circles over the picture (speaker) and two
  people one above the other (split) only when the person asks for everyone to stay on the screen (Tarm, 10-29, on a
  clip with circles: "ถ้าจะคัต ต้อง cut สลับหน้า ไม่ใช่วาง layout แบบนี้"). Never leave a person who speaks off the
  screen: if a shot shows the wrong person, change who is shown then.
- Captions on: in a conversation the words are the clip. Plain captions, never lit word by word. When the picture
  already shows words of its own, as an edited episode does, keep captions on anyway: the program leaves out each
  line said while the picture's own words show, because two sets of words fight (EP 0, 10-30: its own subtitle and
  ours, one over the other), and its own words come and go, so the rest still needs ours.
- The title names the question or the claim in a few words, in the clip's language; a number in it must be one that
  was said.
- Pictures: when they name something a picture can show (a ghost in a ghost story, a crowd of viewers, a
  mountain of money), a picture of it over the top on the second it is said, 1 to 3 in a clip, each a new thing,
  so the top of the frame changes as the talk moves on (Tarm, 10-30: "ตอนที่พูดถึงเรื่องผีก็มีภาพผี").
- Look: none, or warm for faces. No slow push: the cuts carry the pace.
- Sounds: none, or a pop on a sticker. The voices sell the moment.

## Checked at the end

- the captions are words that were said
- nothing private can be read on the screen, a viewer's handle included
- numbers on screen were said
- the length is inside the Reel limit, and the clip does not start silent or black
