# Trip or event clips

After the clip, the viewer knows where this was, what happened there and how it felt, told in the order that
tells it best.

## Choosing and ordering

- Open on the clip that says where we are, or the one that grabs the eye most: the viewer decides in the first
  two seconds.
- Then follow the order the clips were shot in, unless another order tells it better. When a clip moves out of
  that order, say why.
- One piece shows one thing happening. Two to four seconds is often enough; keep a longer piece only while
  something goes on all through it, such as a game played to its end or someone speaking to the camera.
- Cut a piece where the picture shakes, turns away or goes dark, and start it where the action starts.
- Leave a clip out when a better one shows the same thing (two phones at the same moment: keep the better),
  when it is shaky, dark or blurred all through, or when nothing in it matters to the story. Say which.
- Close on the clip that ends the day or the event: a result, the last thing seen, people leaving.
- When someone talks to the camera through the clips (a review or a talk told over several takes), what is said
  is the story: choose the lines that tell it, from each clip's list, and give each piece the seconds around them.
  One clip can give several pieces. Open on the line that says what this is; never use a stretch the list marks cut.
- A product review told over several takes comes with a cut the program made in the review order. Use it as it is,
  with words over its first piece, unless the person asked for something else.

## The words on screen

- A short line over a piece whose meaning the picture does not make plain: where we are, what this is, what
  happens next. Not every piece needs one; a line stays on until the next one, so one line can cover a few pieces.
- A line tells what the viewer cannot see for themselves. A line naming what anyone can see in the picture
  ("บรรยากาศร้านและผู้คน", "ถนนคึกคัก", "ป้ายสีสัน") is read instead of watched: leave the piece without one.
- Say only what the pictures show or the people say. A name (a place, a brand, an event) goes in only as it is
  written on a sign, a banner or a screen in the pictures, or as it is said.
- Write in the person's language, short enough to read in the time it is on.
- Say it the way people who speak that language say it, never word by word from another language: handwriting is
  เขียนด้วยมือ, not เขียนข้อมือ (a wrist). The same goes for the title, the post and your answer.
- Read each line once more as a viewer who was not there. When it can mean something else, say it another way:
  "Pokémon Café ปิดวัน" reads as the café closing; "จบวันที่ Pokémon Café" says the day ended there.

## Rules

- Every second is moving video. A picture that holds still is a frozen screen and never goes in.
- Every face is kept as it is: the people at a trip or an event are part of the day. Blur someone only when the
  person asks, such as "blur the man in white at 12 s": look at that second's faces, numbered, and blur that one.
- Captions are only for people speaking to the camera, in their own words.

## The clip

- The picture fills the frame. A clip shot lying down is cropped upright around the face the program finds, or
  around the focus you give its piece: the product and the hands showing it, and the face when it fits. Give whole
  only for a screen or a wide view where every part matters: it is shown small over a blurred copy of itself.
- In a product review the product leads, never the face: give every clip shot lying down the box around the product
  and the hands showing it (for a screen, the part of it being talked about). The program fills the upright frame
  around it, pushes in on it slowly while it is shown, and puts the person's face in a small circle beside it.
- The title says what the day or the event was, in the clip's language.

## Checked at the end

- every clip is used, or left out with a reason
- the words on screen say only what is seen or said
- the length is inside the Reel limit, and the clip does not start black or silent
